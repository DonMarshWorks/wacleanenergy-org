A coalition of fifteen environmental organizations including the Washington
Clean Energy Coalition, Sierra Club, and several 350.org chapters asked the
Washington Utilities and Transportation Commission to reject Puget Sound
Energy's Clean Energy Implementation Plan and All-Source RFP. The letter
argues PSE's resource planning relies on temperature data from a colder
climate era, dismisses the possibility of becoming a summer-peaking utility,
and over-invests in wind while shorting solar and storage.

- PSE acknowledged it cannot produce climate-aware load forecasts until
  mid-2022 — after the CEIP is finalized — a position Commissioner Rendahl
  called "basically untenable."
- 94% of "Loss of Load" scenarios in PSE's modeling come from pre-1972
  weather data, when winters were significantly colder than recent decades.
- Coalition analysis of UW/SEATAC data shows the coldest of the cold rising
  0.7°F per decade and the hottest of the hot at 0.5°F per decade — faster
  than the average trend.
- PSE plans 50% of 2025 resource acquisitions in wind vs. 20–35% for
  PacifiCorp and Portland General Electric, with only 3% in storage vs.
  13–16% for those peer utilities.
- Pacific Northwest National Laboratory research cited in the letter shows
  climate change nearly eliminates winter shortfalls but greatly increases
  summer shortfalls due to reduced hydropower combined with higher cooling
  demand.
