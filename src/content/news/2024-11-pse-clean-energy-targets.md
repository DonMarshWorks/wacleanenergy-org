WCEC strongly opposed Puget Sound Energy's proposal to lower its interim
clean energy targets under the Clean Energy Transformation Act. The
filing argues the slippage widens the gap to the mandated 80%-by-2030
goal and shifts the cost of PSE's inaction onto customers.

- PSE proposed cutting its 2024 target from 59% → 48% and its 2025
  target from 63% → 48%, well below the trajectory needed for 80% by
  2030.
- PSE blamed low hydro years and tech-company competition for clean
  resources — risks the company had been warned about for years.
- PSE acknowledged the interim targets were reachable with $200M in
  short-term market purchases, but argued doing so would not help the
  2030 goal.
- WCEC names many cost-effective options PSE delayed: demand response,
  distributed energy, virtual power plants, EV integration, time-of-use
  rates, and electrical efficiency.
- The filing asks the UTC to reject the proposal and hold PSE
  accountable for its slow rate of innovation and inadequate planning.
