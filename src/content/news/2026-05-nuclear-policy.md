WCEC wrote to Governor Bob Ferguson to share concerns about the
development of nuclear energy facilities in Washington — particularly
Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) — and to request the governor's positions
on six key questions covering cost, safety, waste storage, and tribal
opposition. The letter argues Washington can meet its climate goals
using solar, wind, batteries, hydro, and geothermal, without the cost
and risks of nuclear.

- Nuclear energy costs roughly 3–5× more than other sources; $25M in
  Washington taxpayer money was allocated in 2023–25 to support SMR
  development, and a 2026 tax abatement supports a Richland nuclear fuel
  plant.
- SMRs require fuel enriched up to 20% (vs 5% for large reactors),
  producing spent fuel that is more dangerous and lacking any long-term
  US storage solution.
- The Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation have
  stated they do NOT support SMR deployment at or near the Hanford Site.
- Federal regulations covering 790 pages of nuclear-safety rules —
  including groundwater, drinking-water, and worker-radiation
  protections — were recently eliminated.
- Alternatives are advancing rapidly: solar and wind are now the
  cheapest new energy, 200,000+ MW of Pacific Northwest renewable
  projects await BPA sign-off, and battery and geothermal capacity are
  expanding fast.
