WCEC and Vashon Climate Action Group challenged Puget Sound Energy's
Customer Benefit Indicator (CBI) methodology in its Draft Clean Energy
Implementation Plan. The filing demonstrates how scoring choices
systematically eliminate substation batteries from PSE's preferred
portfolio despite the company actively touting a similar battery
installation in a Bainbridge substation.

- PSE's three-point CBI scoring scale (0, 1, 2) obliterates meaningful
  differences between DER programs — 19 of 22 programs receive identical
  scores on the first four CETA-aligned metrics.
- Only 3 of the 11 weighted metrics actually differentiate programs in
  the final ranking, hinging the methodology on factors arguably
  peripheral to CETA's core purpose.
- PSE Substation Batteries received the lowest possible scores on
  Affordability, Reduced cost impacts, Clean energy jobs, and Outage
  reduction — while residential, commercial, and utility-scale batteries
  score the highest on those same metrics.
- PSE simultaneously touts a 3.3 MW Bainbridge Island substation battery
  as providing $330,000/year in savings, frequency response, and deferred
  substation investment.
- Corrected scoring proposed by WCEC would rank substation batteries
  equal to or better than 15 of the other 21 DER programs.
